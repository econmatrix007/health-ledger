Morning Check-in

A steady routine makes readings comparable from day to day. A common pattern: (1) weigh after rising and using the bathroom, (2) take morning medicines, (3) wait the interval your care team recommends (the ledger assumes 2 hours), then measure BP, pulse, and SpO₂, (4) if the first BP reads high, rest quietly and retake in 15–20 minutes.

trips today

Two independent estimates. Trip count: small = half, normal = 1, large = 1.5 times your normal trip. Fluid balance: the day's logged fluid intake minus the change in weight from that morning to the next morning (1 lb ≈ 454 mL), so it fills in after the next day's weigh-in. Food water and breath/sweat losses roughly cancel, so expect ±300 to 500 mL of error; fever, heavy sweating, or missed fluid entries widen it. When the two estimates agree, trust them more. To calibrate your normal trip, measure a few once with a kitchen measuring pitcher. Your care team's thresholds override anything here. On exercise days with before/after weights on the Exercise tab, measured sweat is subtracted from the balance estimate.

Exercise

Your routine and timing

Exercise-day morning

  1. Weigh, take morning medicines, eat breakfast.
  2. At 2 hours: rest 5 minutes, then BP, pulse and SpO₂. Recheck in 15 to 20 minutes if high.
  3. Then exercise. If you take a water pill, choose a route or indoor equipment near a bathroom for the hours it is working.
  4. Afterward, stand up slowly. Blood pressure medicines and water pills can make you light-headed.

Exercise lowers BP for several hours, so exercising before you measure would make the reading look better than it really is. If you walk right after your pills instead, do it the same way each time and tick "Exercised before this reading" on the Morning Check-in tab.

Each 40-minute session

5 minWarm-up at an easy pace
30 minSteady effort, RPE 11 to 13: you can talk, not sing
5 minCool-down and gentle stretching. Don't stop suddenly; let your pulse settle.

Go by effort and breathing, not a pulse target. Beta-blockers and some other heart medicines keep your heart rate from climbing the way it normally would.

Leave a rest day between sessions. Cold mornings raise BP, so exercise indoors when it is cold out. If you have a fluid limit, don't drink beyond it for workouts without asking your care team.

Stop and call your care team for chest pain or pressure, unusual shortness of breath, light-headedness, palpitations, or a pulse that stays unusually slow. Call 911 if chest pain doesn't ease with rest. Get your doctor's or rehab team's OK before moving up a month in your plan.

Backup, export & restore

Everything you log is saved in this browser on this device; nothing is sent to a server. Export a backup now and then for your own files.

"Erase logged data" removes daily logs, exercise, notes and articles on this device, so it asks you to type ERASE. Recipes, medicines, the Reading List and settings stay. Erase on each device, or the next sync file you load brings the data back.

Sync between your Mac, iPhone and iPad

Your ledger lives in this browser on this device. To share it across devices, keep one sync file in an iCloud Drive folder named Health Ledger. Loading a sync file merges it with what is here: nothing logged on this device is thrown away.

The routine
  1. Finish on one device: Save sync file to iCloud Drive › Health Ledger (on iPhone or iPad choose Save to Files; replace the old file if asked).
  2. Start on another device: Load sync file from the same folder before you log anything.
  3. On a Mac using Chrome or Edge, Link a sync file once and the ledger reads and writes it automatically.

Deleting an entry on one device does not delete it from a sync file another device made. Sync right after deleting.
